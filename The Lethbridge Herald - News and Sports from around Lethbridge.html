Orange shirts, open hearts for reconciliation


By Lethbridge Herald on October 1, 2026.

University of Lethbridge students and staff gather in orange shirts Monday to mark Truth and Reconciliation Week, which featured activities focused on learning, reflection and Indigenous culture. (HERALD PHOTO BY ALEXANDRA NOAD)

Alexandra Noad

LETHBRIDGE HERALD

Local Journalism Initiative Reporter

The University of Lethbridge hosted various activities this week to help students and staff understand the importance of truth and reconciliation.

On Monday the university kicked off the week in a meaningful way with a smudge and prayer by Elder in Residence Frances First Charger.

The Uhall Atrium quickly filled with students and faculty adorned in orange shirts, a sight Annette Fox-BruisedHead, vice-provost for Iniskim Indigenous Services, says was heartwarming to see.

“We know it is very difficult for Indigenous people to carry that emotional burden and so watching everyone wanting to be a part of it allows us as Indigenous people here on campus … that safety, that trust, that we recognize the engagement is sincere and they want (not only) Indigenous peoples, but all peoples to be successful.”

The University of Lethbridge has been a trailblazer in promoting truth and reconciliation, being one of the first universities in Canada to offer Aboriginal Studies, now known as Indigenous Studies. The university has also included the Blackfoot language in many of its spaces and programs, allowing students to see their own identity within the institution.

Fox-BruisedHead says the spirit of reconciliation is evident on campus and encouraged all students listening to her speech Monday to keep the spirit of reconciliation alive by going through their programs with open hearts and minds.

“This space allows for creativity to happen in their research and their work and so then they can take that out to whatever profession, whatever job, families they have … they’re going to have a better understanding of why certain things in our country are the way they are.”
While many believe there is still a long road ahead, Fox-BruisedHead says it’s important to also celebrate how far we’ve come.

“I know it’s taken many years to get here; I never thought in my day I would see that, (and) I’m sure when I talk to my 85-year-old dad, he would say the same,” she says. “He never thought this would ever come, that his truth would be recognized and acknowledged for what it really was.
