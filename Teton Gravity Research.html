Mt. Bachelor Locks In December 4 Opening Day

Mt. Bachelor sets December 4 as opening day for the 2026/27 ski season. Read about early snowfall, resort updates, and what to expect in Central Oregon.

Central Oregon skiers and riders can start counting down the days. Mt. Bachelor has set December 4 as its official opening day for the 2026/27 season, targeting a fresh start after a challenging winter last year.

The 2025/26 season brought tough conditions to the Pacific Northwest. Bachelor picked up just 140 inches of total snow, marking its lowest snowfall in 50 years. That brought a delayed opening and an early wrap to operations. However, resort leadership and local crews are looking forward as early dusting hits the upper mountain.

Early Flurries Signal a Seasonal Shift

Temps are dropping across the Cascade Range, and light snow is already dusting the volcano’s summit. GM John Merriman noted that early weather patterns are showing classic Pacific Northwest cloud coverage lingering over the peak. That is a promising sign for cold temps and steady Pacific storms.

With an average seasonal snowfall around 400 inches, the mountain is preparing for a return to form. Ops crews are running lift maintenance, dialing in snowmaking systems, and preparing the terrain for opening week.

Deep Roots in the PNW

Mt. Bachelor holds a special place in freeride culture. Known for its massive natural wind lip features, volcanic rime formations, and open bowl terrain, it remains a favorite stop for TGR athletes cruising through Oregon. From chasing storm cycles through the hemlocks to catching spring park laps, Bachelor delivers a unique layout for high-speed riding.

If weather models hold and early systems keep rolling in, December 4 will mark the start of another winter full of storm days, cold laps, and deep Pacific Northwest turns.
